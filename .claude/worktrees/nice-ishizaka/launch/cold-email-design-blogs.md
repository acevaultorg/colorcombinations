Each outlet's current editor and submission process changes over time —
always check the outlet's live site before copy-pasting contacts from
any list, including this one. Sidebar.io and CSS Weekly are historically
the easiest wins and highest ROI-per-minute. Start there.

**Body:**

> I think fits your readership.
>
> **Project:** https://colorcombinations.org
>
> It's a web-native reconstruction of *A Dictionary of Color
> Combinations* — Sanzo Wada's 1933 six-volume reference book, 348
> numbered color plates pulled from traditional Japanese textile,
> printing, and costume practice. The original book is out of print;
> the 2010 Seigensha reprint is expensive and hard to find outside

>
> **Why I think it's [Outlet]-appropriate:**
>
> - It's a case study in static-site design at scale — 612 pages, zero
>   runtime JS per page, Astro 5 + Tailwind v4.

>   generators, the constraint argument) that I wrote up here:
>   https://colorcombinations.org/blog/how-to-use-sanzo-wada-in-modern-design/

>   link to it or use the exports in real projects without signing up.

> dataset; curatorial and editorial layer is mine.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> Paulo

## Etiquette

- **Personalize the first line.** Referencing a specific recent article
  you actually read is the difference between a 2% response rate and a
  20% response rate.
- **Never follow up more than once.** One bump email, 7 days later,
  then let it go. Design editors get hundreds of pitches a week; being
  remembered as the polite founder is worth more than the single
  placement.
- **Attach nothing.** Include images only as URLs. Attached files get
  auto-spam-filtered by most editorial inboxes.
- **Don't pitch the same outlet twice within 60 days.**

## Tracking

Keep a spreadsheet: Outlet | Contact | Date sent | Response | Link
(if placed). Some editors circle back 3 months later when they have a
slot; the spreadsheet is how you remember why they might know the name.

## If you get a "no, but"

"No, but I'll keep this in mind" is the most common response.
Treat it as a yes-with-delay. Don't chase it — just send an update
email when there's a meaningful next beat (a major new feature, a
milestone like "now 1,000 palettes"). Editors remember founders who
respect their bandwidth.
