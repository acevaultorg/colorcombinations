# Social thread — Bluesky · Mastodon · Threads · X

**Twitter no longer exists** — it rebranded to X in July 2023. More
importantly for a designer-audience launch in 2026: the active design
and tech-writing community has largely moved off X to Bluesky, Mastodon,
and (to a lesser extent) Threads. Organic reach on X without paid boost
is poor. Prioritize accordingly.

**Recommended post order** (each 30–60 min apart to look native, not
syndicated):

1. **Bluesky** — strongest design + tech-writing community in 2026.
   Threads are native, no character gymnastics.
2. **Mastodon** — smaller but deeply engaged. Post from a design-adjacent
   instance if you have one; otherwise `mastodon.social` is fine.
3. **Threads** — Instagram-adjacent audience, pulls non-designer eyeballs
   + strong algorithmic boost for new accounts.
4. **X** — optional. Only worth posting if you have an existing audience
   there; cold accounts get buried.

**Best time to post:** Tue–Thu, 9–11am or 2–4pm in your primary audience's
timezone. Avoid weekends.

**Image strategy:** each post referencing a palette should include an
image. The site's OG SVGs at `colorcombinations.org/og/{slug}.svg` render
at 1200×630 and are designed for exactly this. Screenshot the OG image
(not the whole page) for maximum clarity.

The thread is 10 posts. Post 1 is the hook + image. Posts 2–8 are the
body. Post 9 is the ask. Post 10 is the receipts. Each is under 280
characters so it works on every platform without edits.

## Post 3

> The book pairs two or three colors per plate, 348 plates total. Each
> one is a specific textile, a specific season, a specific social
> context.
>
> Nothing random. Every entry was used, on real materials, by real
> people.
>
> This is the opposite of a palette generator.

## Post 4

> Generators give you infinite novelty. Great for iteration. Bad for
> decisions.
>
> A dictionary gives you constraints. You scan, you find the one that
> fits, you commit.
>
> Finding is harder than generating. Generators do the easy half and
> hand you the hard half.

## Platform-specific notes

### Bluesky

- Threads post natively. Click-to-reply chains each post below the
  previous.
- Drop the "🧵" marker in post 1 — Bluesky's threading is visual, the
  emoji adds no info.
- Add the thread to any Art & Design starter-pack you belong to after
  publishing.
- No hashtags needed; Bluesky search indexes keywords.

### Mastodon

- Post from a design-aligned instance if you have one. Otherwise
  `mastodon.social` works.
- Add hashtags in post 1 only: `#ColorTheory #Design #WebDesign
  #Typography`.
- Use a content warning (CW) only if the instance culture expects it;
  most design instances don't.

### Threads

- Keep the thread as-is — character limits are generous, threading is
  native.
- Threads' algorithm rewards engagement in the first 30 minutes more
  aggressively than other platforms. Be ready to reply quickly.

### X (formerly Twitter)

- Worth posting only if you have an existing audience on X. Cold accounts
  under the current algorithm get buried.
- If you post, use the word "thread" in post 1 instead of "🧵" — X
  suppresses posts with lots of non-ASCII characters.
- Pin the thread's first post for 24 hours to maximize impressions.

## Do NOT auto-cross-post

Don't use a syndication tool (Buffer, Hypefury, etc.). Each platform's
audience reads differently — a native post outperforms a syndicated one
every time. Copy-paste manually, 30–60 min between platforms.
