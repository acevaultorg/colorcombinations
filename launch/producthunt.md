## Description (markdown supported)

> **The Dictionary of Color Combinations** is a modern rebuild of Sanzo
> Wada's 1933 Japanese color reference — the six-volume book that
> quietly became an underground classic for designers, colorists, and
> anyone who's tried to pick a palette that doesn't feel generated.
>
> **Why this exists**
>
> Palette generators give you infinite novelty. That's the easy half.
> The hard half is picking one, defending it in a client review, and
> committing. A curated dictionary solves the decision differently:
> 348 plates, each one used on real materials by real people. Your job
> shrinks from "invent" to "find what fits".
>
> **What's in the archive**
>
> - All 348 Wada plates, one page each, with historical context
> - 30 editorial deep-dives on traditional shikisai (色彩) names — 紅
>   kurenai, 藍 ai, 萌黄 moegi, etc.
> - 8 themed collections — Websites, Branding, Autumn, Minimalist,
>   Indigo, Bold, Heian, Spring — organized around real briefs, not
>   color theory
> - A reverse color dictionary: 211 named colors × the palettes that
>   contain each one
> - Per-palette WCAG contrast matrix for accessibility-aware pairing
> - One-click export: hex list, Tailwind config, CSS variables, JSON
> - Optional $3-5 pay-what-you-want bundle with Figma tokens for the
>   workflow-focused
>
> **Technical**
>
> Astro 5 + Tailwind v4 + TypeScript strict, hosted on Cloudflare Pages.
> Zero runtime JS per page. Static site, works on any browser, loads on
> slow connections.
>
> **Credit**
>
> Source data is the MIT-licensed
> [mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations](https://github.com/mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations)
> dataset. Curatorial overlay, editorial entries, site design, and
> export rails are original work. Full ethics note at
> colorcombinations.org/about.

> I built this over a few weeks of evenings because I wanted a color

## First 24 hours playbook

PH's algorithm heavily weights engagement in the first 4 hours. To
maximize:

1. **Post exactly at 12:01 AM Pacific.** The 24-hour ranking window
   starts then.
2. **Reply to every comment within 15 minutes for the first 4 hours.**
   Engagement begets engagement.
3. **Don't ask for upvotes directly.** PH detects upvote-begging and
   ghost-bans. But DO share on Twitter ("I'm launching on Product Hunt
   today — would love your feedback") and let natural support roll in.
4. **Cross-pollinate with HN if you're launching both.** But don't
   launch HN and PH the same day — the traffic spikes don't
   compound; they fragment. Space them a week apart.
