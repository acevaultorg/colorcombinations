## Post

> I spent a few weeks of evenings rebuilding a 90-year-old color
> reference book for the modern web, and I want to share why it matters
> for brand work.
>
> In 1933 a Japanese painter named Sanzo Wada published six volumes
> titled *A Dictionary of Color Combinations*. 348 numbered plates,
> each pairing two or three colors drawn from traditional textiles,
> woodblock prints, and costume. It quietly became an underground
> reference for designers and colorists — cited in art schools, quoted
> by costume departments, passed around as photocopies in studios.
>
> The original is out of print. The 2010 Seigensha reprint is expensive
> and hard to find outside Japan.
>
> So I rebuilt the catalog as an open web archive.
>
> 👉 https://colorcombinations.org
>
> What's there:
>
> – All 348 Wada plates, one page each, with historical context
> – 30 hand-written editorial entries on traditional shikisai names
>   (紅 kurenai, 藍 ai, 萌黄 moegi, and others)
> – A reverse color dictionary — 211 named colors, each linking to
>   every palette that uses it
> – Per-palette WCAG contrast matrix so pairs are accessibility-
>   usable before you commit
> – One-click export to hex list, Tailwind config, CSS variables, or
>   JSON
> – Pinterest-ready OG previews on every palette URL
>
> Why it matters for brand work specifically:
>
> Palette generators are great for ideation. They are bad for
> decisions. When you need to defend a color choice to a client,
> "the generator produced it" is not a defense. "This is plate 027
> from Wada's 1933 catalog, used in Edo-era textiles, and here is the
> historical context" is a defense.
>
> A curated dictionary turns palette work from invention into
> selection. Selection is faster, more defensible, and — critically —
> more memorable to the client.
>
> The whole thing is free. If it saves you time on a brief, pass it
> along.
>
> Longer write-up on why dictionaries beat generators for professional
> work: https://colorcombinations.org/blog/how-to-use-sanzo-wada-in-modern-design/
>
> Stack notes for the builders: Astro 5 + Tailwind v4 + TypeScript
> strict, Cloudflare Pages, zero runtime JS per page, 614 static
> pages. Source data from the MIT-licensed
> mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations dataset — full credit
> on the About page.

## Anticipated comments — pre-written responses

### "How do you handle cultural appropriation concerns?"

> The traditional Japanese color names (紅, 藍, 萌黄, etc.) are part of
> the cultural commons, in continuous use for centuries. They
> aren't Wada's invention. The site documents the tradition, credits
> sources, and includes an explicit ethics note on /about. I'm not
> the first person to rebuild this dataset; the mattdesl project has
> been on GitHub for years.

### "Can my team actually use these for client work?"

> Yes. Hex values are facts, not copyrightable. Names are cultural
> commons. The archive's editorial writing is the only thing I hold,
> and none of that needs to show up in your deliverable. Use the
> exports in any project, commercial or personal.

### "Is this a pitch for a service?"

> No. There's a $3-$5 pay-what-you-want bundle on /shop that packages
> the data as Figma tokens + format files, but only for people who
> prefer the zip-on-disk workflow. The archive itself is and will stay
> free.
