# Twitter / X / Threads / Mastodon / Bluesky thread — ready to paste

**Post on:** Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11am or 2–4pm in your primary audience's
timezone. Avoid weekends.

**Image strategy:** each tweet with a palette reference should include a
screenshot of that palette's page. The open-graph SVGs at
`colorcombinations.org/og/{slug}.svg` are designed for this exact use — they
render as 1200×630 cards. Screenshot the OG image (not the whole page) for
maximum compression and clarity.

The thread is 10 tweets. Tweet 1 is the hook + image. Tweets 2–8 are the
body. Tweet 9 is the ask. Tweet 10 is the receipts.

Each tweet is ≤ 280 characters. Pasted as-is.

## Tweet 2

> Sanzo Wada (和田 三造, 1883–1967) was a painter, costume designer, and
> color researcher. He won the first Academy Award for Best Costume Design
> in 1953.
>
> His "Dictionary of Color Combinations" (配色事典) is the most cited
> Japanese color reference of the 20th century.

---

## Tweet 3

> The book pairs two or three colors per plate, 348 plates total. Each one
> is a specific textile, a specific season, a specific social context.
>
> Nothing random. Every entry was used, on real materials, by real people.
>
> This is the opposite of a palette generator.

---

## Tweet 4

> Generators give you infinite novelty. Great for iteration. Bad for
> decisions.
>
> A dictionary gives you constraints. You scan, you find the one that
> fits, you commit.
>
> Finding is harder than generating. Generators do the easy half and hand
> you the hard half.

## Mastodon / Bluesky variants

Same thread. Same images. Drop the "🧵" marker — those platforms don't
have the implicit thread convention.

**Mastodon:** Post at `@yourhandle@mastodon.social` (or your instance). Add
hashtags in the first post only: `#colortheory #design #webdesign #typography`.

**Bluesky:** Threads post natively. Add to an "Art & Design" starter-pack
if you belong to any.

## Cross-post order

1. Twitter / X (widest reach, most algorithmic uplift)
2. Mastodon (design crowd lives here now, especially after Twitter's decline)
3. Bluesky (newer, more engaged, smaller)
4. Threads (Instagram-adjacent audience; may pull non-designer eyeballs)

Wait 30–60 minutes between each to avoid the "bot spam" signature some
platforms penalize. Don't auto-cross-post via a tool — each platform's
culture is different enough that a native post outperforms a syndicated one.
